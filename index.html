What is Phishing?

Phishing is a form of cyberattack where attackers impersonate legitimate organizations (banks, employers, cloud providers) to deceive individuals into disclosing sensitive data, such as usernames, passwords, credit card numbers, or installing malware.

⚠️ Common Phishing Vectors

  • Email Phishing: Mass emails mimicking reputable entities.
  • Spear Phishing: Highly targeted attacks on specific individuals or companies.
  • Whaling: Targeting high-profile executives (CEOs, CFOs).
  • Smishing & Vishing: SMS texts or voice phone calls claiming urgency.

📊 Why Phishing Succeeds

Attackers exploit human psychology rather than software vulnerabilities. Over 90% of data breaches originate from a single phishing email.

By training to spot red flags, you become the primary firewall of your organization.

Recognizing Fake Websites & Spoofed Domains

Cybercriminals build pixel-perfect clones of login pages (Google, Microsoft, Netflix, Banks). Here is how to tell them apart:

❌ Attacker Deceptions

  • Typosquatting: www.paypa1.com or www.netflix-verify.com
  • Subdomain Trickery: login.microsoft.com.attacker-server.net
  • Punycode/Lookalikes: Using Cyrillic characters that look identical to Latin letters (e.g., gооgle.com).
  • The "HTTPS" Myth: Having a padlock 🔒 only means traffic is encrypted, not that the site owner is trustworthy!

✅ Verification Rules

  • Look at the Root Domain directly before the top-level domain (`.com`, `.org`).
  • Never click links in unexpected emails—navigate to the service manually via your bookmarks.
  • Use modern browsers with built-in phishing filters (SmartScreen, Safe Browsing).
  • Always enable Two-Factor Authentication (2FA/MFA).

Social Engineering Tactics

Social engineering is the psychological manipulation of people into performing actions or divulging confidential information.

1. Urgency & Panic ⏳

"Your account will be permanently deleted unless you verify now." Rushing bypasses critical thinking.

2. Impersonating Authority 👔

Emails pretending to be from the CEO, IT director, or tax agency (IRS/FBR) demanding wire transfers or gift cards.

3. Curiosity & Greed 🎁

"You have won an Amazon \$500 voucher!" or "Attached: Q4 Salary & Bonus Report.pdf.exe".

4. Fear & Coercion 🚨

"We have recorded illegal activity on your computer. Pay bitcoin within 24 hours to avoid police arrest."

Best Practices & Defense Playbook

🛡️ The Golden Rules of Anti-Phishing

  1. SLIM Test: Check the Sender, Links, Information requested, and Motivation/Tone.
  2. Never Send Credentials via Email: Legitimate IT staff will NEVER ask for your password.
  3. Enable Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA/2FA): Even if an attacker steals your password, they cannot log in without your physical phone or authenticator app.
  4. Use Password Managers: Password managers will not autofill your credentials on a fake URL.
  5. Report Suspicious Messages: Use your organization's "Report Phishing" button immediately.
